# Keep a turn's snapshot to the specifications, and bound the turns

An undo snapshot copied each chart with `{...chart, transforms: [...]}`, and a stored chart carries its resolved `table` — so every turn that changed something carried up to eight full tables of 500 rows by 32 columns. `record.turns` was appended to without any bound (`MAX_TURNS_IN_PROMPT` bounds the prompt, not the record), and `store.write` serializes the whole record on every commit, of which `reading.ts` makes one per read. A wide source with eight charts and a dozen changing turns put tens of megabytes on disk, and a chart on the ten-second live-update interval rewrote all of it every ten seconds, with the unbounded turn array going on the wire on every mutation too. The file's own header claimed a snapshot "costs what a list of specifications costs" when it cost the tables as well.

- `snapshot` in `src/visualizations/undo.ts` now keeps what to draw and drops what was drawn: the id, the data reference, the interval and the specification. `VisualizationChartSpec` in the protocol is that shape, named, so the type says what a snapshot is rather than a cast saying it.
- `reverted` re-resolves each restored chart through `drawn` against the record's current datasets, so a chart taken back is drawn from the data its own specification names rather than from a copy of a table that has been re-read since. The manager case asserts the restored chart has its rows and its read time again.
- The store's guard learned the new shape: `isChartSpecList` in `chart-record.ts` validates a chart specification — the grammar, the data reference, the interval, the transformations — and requires the absence of a table, so a snapshot naming a column or a step the grammar has not got still cannot be read back. `isChart` is now `isTable` plus that same specification guard, so the two cannot drift.
- `kept` in `undo.ts` bounds the record at forty turns, ten times what a prompt carries, dropping the oldest. An unbounded turn list is the same mistake `MAX_ROWS` and `MAX_TRANSFORMS` exist to prevent, paid on every keystroke in every tab rather than once. The oldest goes, and with it anything undoable, because an undo outliving its turn would have nothing to restore.

`src/visualizations/undo.test.ts` is new: a snapshot keeps the specification and drops the table, the transforms are copied rather than shared, the summary names a removal, a change and an addition but not a rename, and the turn bound keeps the most recent forty. `store.test.ts` round-trips a snapshot that carries no table — which is the case that failed first, because the store's guard still demanded one.

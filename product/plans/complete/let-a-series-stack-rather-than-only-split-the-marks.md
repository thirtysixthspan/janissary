# Let a series stack rather than only split the marks

A `series` column splits the marks into one each, which is a grouped bar, and nothing in the grammar said how the series combine — so "stacked by region" and "show me the share" were both refused by a host that could only group, and the model answered with adjacent bars that look like a stack to a reader who is not looking carefully. Vega-Lite makes stacking the default for a bar with a non-position unaggregated channel and offers `zero`/`normalize`/`center`; Plot does the same through an implicit `stackY` and a `proportion` reducer.

- `stack?: 'zero' | 'normalize'` on the chart shape, in the wire type, the shared contract and the client, validated by the grammar. `center` is deliberately absent: a diverging stack is a bar chart of signed values, which the aggregate and the axis already say.
- `validateChart` refuses a stack without a series, and on a pie or a scatter — a pie has no second dimension to split and a scatter has no bands to stack in, so accepting one there would store a field that does nothing.
- `Point` in `web/src/plugins/visualizations/chart/points.ts` gains `from`, the bottom of the mark, alongside `value`, its top. A mark drawn from the axis has them equal; a stacked segment does not. Carrying both means the bar renderer has one case rather than two, and the y domain has one thing to be a domain over.
- `stacked` walks the points in band order and carries a running total per band, dividing by the band\x27s total for `normalize` — and it accumulates the *normalized* value, not the raw one, so a share stack\x27s segments end at one rather than at the band\x27s total. A negative value stacks downwards from the band it started in, which is what a diverging bar is.
- A stacked chart gives every series the whole band rather than a slot within it, since a segment with gaps in it is a slice of a smaller bar than the chart says. `extentOf` now takes the bottoms as well as the tops, because a stack whose segments were each within the domain would have its top segment drawn off the top of the plot.
- The caption and the sentence a chart leaves behind both say which of the two a stack is: a share read as a total is a chart saying the wrong thing, and the caption is the only place that can say it.

`web/src/plugins/visualizations/chart/points.test.ts` covers a zero stack, a normalize stack whose segments sum to one per band with the running total in normalized units, and an unstacked chart left exactly as it was. `src/visualizations/chart-spec.test.ts` covers the grammar and the two refusals. `VisualizationChartCard.test.tsx` covers the caption. The spec gains a paragraph.

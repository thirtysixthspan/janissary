# Detect the anomalies and the unexpected values a chart would otherwise hide

The tab drew whatever it was given and reported nothing about it, so it could answer "what does this data say" and never "what is wrong with it" — which is the question a monitoring-shaped tool exists for. Tableau Pulse states the shape this follows: "standardised, deterministic statistical models" detect facts that are guaranteed to be accurate first, and the language is written afterwards, grounded in those facts. ThoughtSpot's equivalent is to intercept a question beginning with "Why" and hand it to a change-analysis engine rather than to the chart drawer. Neither asks a model to notice something.

Four pure functions in `src/visualizations/insights-statistics.ts` and four detectors in `src/visualizations/insights.ts`:

- **An outlier**, by Tukey's fences: a value beyond one and a half interquartile ranges of the middle half. The rule is chosen because it does not move when the value being judged is itself extreme, which is exactly the failure a mean and a standard deviation have.
- **An unexpected value**, against a band two sample spreads either side of the mean of the values *before* it and nothing after — a band that included the excursion would be a band that always contains it.
- **A level that has stepped**, where the two halves are each asked what level their own trend predicts at the boundary between them. Comparing medians instead reports every rising series as a step, because a rising series does sit at a different level in its second half; and the obvious failure of a slope-only detector is a series that steps and never slopes at all, which reads as no change because both its halves are flat.
- **A rate that has changed**, by two least-squares slopes compared by factor, and only along an ordered x column — the halves of a series with no order to them are the first rows and the last rows, and a difference between those is a difference between two arbitrary cuts.

`noticed` in `src/visualizations/charts.ts` rebuilds `record.notices` from the charts whenever they change, which is what makes a live update that has come back down stop reporting the spike, and what removes a chart's finding when the chart goes. The findings reach the browser as `notices` on the window view, rendered above the exchange by `VisualizationChat.tsx` in the ordinary text colour with a rule down the side rather than the error colour a failure uses — a spike is a fact about the data and may be entirely expected, and it must not read as something having gone wrong. They reach the next prompt under a heading that says who made them, so the model is asked what to do about a measurement rather than asked to make one. The reply contract gains `notices` for the model's own reading of the data, kept apart from the host's because one is an opinion and the other is not.

Every notice states the value, the rule it broke and the figures behind the rule; every detector stays silent below a stated minimum (eight values for a distribution, five earlier values before a band, four points per half for a trend); a bounded report says how many it did not report; and a row already named by the fences is not named again by the band.

`src/visualizations/insights.test.ts` covers a planted spike with its exact sentence, an ordinary series, a series too short to speak about, an all-equal series, a step, a rate change, a rise that is not a step, a category x column, the "N more" count, a non-numeric measure, and the no-duplicate-row rule. `src/visualizations/manager.test.ts` covers the live read that removes a finding. `src/visualizations/prompts.test.ts` and `web/src/plugins/visualizations/VisualizationTab.test.tsx` cover the prompt section and the rendering. The spec gains a section of its own.

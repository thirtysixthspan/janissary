# Text selection

**Complexity: 5/10** — no new protocol, no server code, no new dependency, and both consumers of the result already exist and already accept a terminal-sourced selection; the selection highlight, the drag-hook shape, and the buffer-to-text loop are all copied from code already in the repo. What is left is one genuinely new client surface — a frozen overlay over a live terminal, with its own pointer gesture and its own lifetime — landing inside the one hook every terminal in the app mounts through, where a mistake breaks typing, copying, or mouse reporting on four surfaces at once.

Backlog text: *"text selection is difficult in harnesses like claude because they dont allow text to remain selected, and instead copy it immediately. add an alternative text selection layer managed by janassiry that selects independent of the underlying subsystems. This should allow selection of text from harnesses that can be feed into copy/paste and chat about this. The key binding could be to hold shift while selecting text using the mouse with the mouse button down."*

A harness tab's body is a live xterm.js terminal driven by a TUI that owns the mouse and repaints continuously. The TUI decides what a drag means: claude reads the drag itself, acts on it, and leaves nothing selected afterwards, so there is no selection to adjust, re-read, copy twice, or hand to anything else. Janissary already has an escape hatch — hold the terminal's forcing modifier while dragging and xterm's own selection service takes the drag instead of the harness (`product/specs/harness.md:388`–`:402`) — but that selection is still emulator state living in the terminal's buffer cells, owned by the same component the harness is repainting underneath, and the modifier is Option on macOS rather than the Shift the backlog text asks for. This feature adds a selection layer Janissary owns outright: Shift+drag freezes the screen into an overlay, text picked there stays picked until the user dismisses it regardless of what the harness draws next, and it feeds the two consumers that already exist for a selection — the clipboard and the conversations plugin's **Chat about this**.

## Design decisions

Settled by the backlog text, by behavior the app already has, or by the user's answers to the two question rounds. Nothing here is a guess, and nothing is left open.

**The gesture is Shift+drag, on every platform, and it is the only one.** The backlog text names it: "hold shift while selecting text using the mouse with the mouse button down". This is already the forcing modifier off macOS (`product/specs/harness.md:392`–`:395`, "Option+drag on macOS, Shift+drag elsewhere"). On macOS it replaces Option+drag: `macOptionClickForcesSelection: true` is **removed** from the terminal's construction (`web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:36`), so xterm's own forcing-modifier selection is off everywhere and Option+drag goes back to being reported to the harness like any other drag. One gesture produces one kind of selection; the alternative — leaving Option+drag alive on macOS — would put two selections with different lifetimes on the same surface, only one of which survives a repaint, and the user would have to remember which modifier produced which.

**The selection is Janissary's, not the harness's and not xterm's.** "an alternative text selection layer managed by janassiry that selects independent of the underlying subsystems" is the whole point of the feature: a selection that neither the harness nor the terminal emulator can revoke by redrawing. Its state, its text, its highlight, and its lifetime are all owned by the app.

**The freeze is the visible screen, and nothing above it.** The snapshot is the terminal's viewport as it stands when the drag starts — not the scrollback behind it. What you see is what you can pick; text that has scrolled off is selected by scrolling the terminal back first and then Shift+dragging, one screenful at a time. This keeps the overlay a still image with no scroll state of its own, which is what the "Out of scope" boundary below already assumes.

**The screen is frozen for the duration.** Shift+pointerdown snapshots the terminal's current text into a Janissary-owned overlay drawn over the viewport, and the user drags over that snapshot. The harness keeps running and its output keeps arriving underneath; none of it moves, changes, or clears what is on the overlay. Clearing the selection drops the overlay and reveals the live screen, caught up. This is the only model in which a repaint provably cannot disturb a held selection — the alternatives (anchoring to live buffer cells, or capturing the text at mouse-up while the highlight stays anchored) both leave the highlight sitting over text that has since changed.

**The tab stays interactive while a selection is held.** Typing still reaches the harness, unchanged — the layer claims no keys for itself beyond **Escape**, and only while a selection is actually held. Escape clears the selection, and so does a plain (unmodified) click anywhere in the terminal. Copying the selection, or sending it to **Chat about this**, leaves it in place, so the same pick can be used twice. A second Shift+drag replaces it, re-freezing from the screen as it stands at that moment.

**Escape is claimed only while a selection is held.** With nothing selected, Escape reaches the PTY exactly as it does today — it is the key a TUI uses for cancel, and a terminal that swallowed it unconditionally would be broken for every harness in the app. This mirrors the rule the copy chord already follows: claimed only while something is selected, so a selection-less Cmd+C reaches the harness unchanged (`web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:66`–`:71`).

**Switching tabs, resizing, and the harness exiting all clear it.** One rule covers the three ways the frozen image can stop matching what it is drawn over: leaving the tab, a resize (the grid the snapshot was taken on no longer exists), and the PTY exiting. A selection is never left frozen over a surface whose geometry or contents have moved on, and there is no case where the user returns to a tab and has to work out why the screen is not updating.

**The context menu's terminal rule is unchanged.** A right-click on a held selection offers **Chat about this** and no **Copy**, exactly as `product/specs/context-menu.md:32` already records for a terminal, and the copy chord stays the way to copy from a terminal. The selection is classified `'terminal'` (`web/src/context-menu/default-menu-target.ts:9`), so `defaultMenuGroups` omits Copy with no change at all (`:64`–`:67`). The menu therefore looks the same whichever way the selection was made, and no spec rule is narrowed.

**The dismissing click is consumed, not forwarded.** A plain click that clears a held selection does nothing else: it is not reported to the harness as a click. While the screen is frozen, the user is pointing at a still image, and a TUI acting on a click aimed at text it has already scrolled away is worse than no click at all.

**No indicator is drawn.** The highlight is the whole affordance: no chip, no hint text, no tint, no border. This matches how the existing forcing-modifier selection is presented today — documented in the spec and the user documentation, and nowhere on screen.

**Every xterm surface gets the layer.** Harness tabs, ssh tabs, interactive PTY takeover, and shell terminal cards all mount through the same `useXterm` hook, which has exactly three production call sites — `web/src/harness/HarnessTab.tsx:39`, `web/src/ShellTab.tsx:28`, and `web/src/shared/transcript/TerminalCard.tsx:25` (ssh tabs share the harness tab surface). The gesture means one thing everywhere in the app. A plain shell that never grabs the mouse never needed the escape hatch, but it behaves consistently with the surfaces that did.

**The overlay renders inside the registered container.** `terminalSelectionText` finds a terminal by asking which registered container `contains` the clicked element (`web/src/shared/terminal/terminal-selection.ts:27`–`:39`). A right-click lands on the overlay, so the overlay must be a descendant of the same element the surface registered — otherwise the context menu resolves no selection and **Chat about this** never appears. This is the single structural requirement the whole menu path depends on.

**Geometry is arithmetic, not a caret API.** The overlay is a fixed grid: the terminal reports `cols` and `rows`, the container reports its own width and height, and a pointer position maps to a cell by division. The snapshot's own line list then supplies the text for a cell range. The editor solves the same-sounding problem a much heavier way — `pointToCol` resolves a column through `caretPositionFromPoint` with a `caretRangeFromPoint` fallback and a tree-walk over the row's spans (`web/src/editor/mouse.ts:48`, `:11`) — because an editor row is proportional, wrapped, and split into syntax spans, so only the browser knows where a column is. A terminal is a fixed monospace grid where division is exact, and taking the editor's route would mean either a cross-feature import (which `ai/guidelines/react-code-organization.md` forbids) or promoting `mouse.ts` to `shared/`, plus rendering the editor's row markup, plus mocking caret APIs that jsdom does not implement. Division needs none of that.

**Both existing consumers of a selection are in scope, and neither changes.** The backlog text names "copy/paste and chat about this". The copy chords are **Cmd+C** (macOS) and **Ctrl+Shift+C** (`web/src/shared/terminal/terminal-keys.ts:26`, `copySelectionChord`); **Chat about this** is the conversations plugin's contributed default-menu entry (`src/plugins/conversations/manifest.ts:13`, `defaultMenu: { label: 'Chat about this' }`), reachable by right-click or by **Cmd+I**/**Ctrl+I** (`web/src/context-menu/useDefaultContextMenu.ts:23`–`:25`, `isChatShortcut`), with `'terminal'` already one of the three selection sources the menu resolves (`web/src/context-menu/default-menu-target.ts:9`, `web/src/context-menu/useDefaultContextMenu.ts:15`–`:21`). Both reach a terminal's selection through the same bridge, so answering that bridge from the layer is the entire integration — no protocol change, no plugin change, no menu change.

**The selection is per-tab and per-surface.** Registration is one terminal per container, innermost wins (`web/src/shared/terminal/terminal-selection.ts:16`, `:34`). A selection belongs to one surface's terminal and is not carried anywhere else.

## Proposed changes

Described in prose; the implementer writes the code. Every placement below follows what an existing terminal concern already does, and every new file is small enough that nothing approaches the 200-line limit.

**Landing order.** The pure model module lands first with its tests, since everything else imports it and it changes no behavior. The layer hook and the overlay component land second. The `useXterm` wiring — the return-shape change, the pointer listeners, the Escape branch, the copy branch, and the bridge registration — lands last, because it is the only step that alters what an existing surface does, and it is the step whose `check-diff` should be read carefully.

**One pure model module.** A single new module under `web/src/shared/terminal/` holds everything that is not React and not a listener, because each half is a handful of lines and neither has a second caller:

- *Snapshot.* Given a `Terminal`, return the **visible** rendered lines as a string array, following `HarnessScreenReader.captureNow`'s loop (`src/harness/screen.ts:66`–`:75`) — `translateToString(true)` per line — with one deliberate difference: that reader iterates the whole of `buffer.active` because it constructs its terminal with `scrollback: 0` (`src/harness/screen.ts:32`), so its buffer *is* the screen. A client terminal has scrollback, so the snapshot reads the `rows` lines starting at the buffer's `viewportY` instead of the whole buffer. Reading the whole buffer is the easiest thing here to get silently wrong: it would put thousands of scrollback lines behind an overlay sized to one screen, and every cell coordinate the geometry resolves would address the wrong line. Trailing empty lines are popped as in the original. It needs no `allowProposedApi` — that option is set at `src/harness/screen.ts:32` because `@xterm/headless@6` gates its `buffer` getter behind `_checkProposedApi()`, which `@xterm/xterm@6` does not, so **the client `Terminal` construction at `web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:29` is left alone**. Adding the option "to be safe" is the obvious wrong move: it opts the app into proposed API everywhere else.
- *Geometry.* A pointer position plus the container's size plus the terminal's `cols`/`rows` maps to a cell, clamped to the grid so a drag that leaves the container still resolves; two cells normalize into an ordered range, so a backwards drag reads the same as a forwards one; a range plus the snapshot's lines resolves to text, joined with newlines, each line's trailing run of spaces trimmed the way a terminal selection is expected to read.

**The layer hook.** A new hook under `web/src/shared/terminal/` binds the model to one surface, following the drag shape `useEditorMouse.ts` already uses for the editor (pointer down starts, window-level move extends, up ends). It installs `pointerdown` on the container element **in the capture phase**, so it runs before xterm's own handlers (xterm attaches to the `.xterm` element `term.open()` creates *inside* the container, and a capture-phase listener on the ancestor runs first). On a Shift+pointerdown it takes the snapshot, records the anchor cell, calls `preventDefault()` and `stopPropagation()` so neither xterm's selection service nor its mouse-reporting path sees the event, and follows the drag with `pointermove`/`pointerup` listeners registered on the window so a release outside the container still ends it. On a plain pointerdown it clears a held selection — consuming that event too — and otherwise does nothing at all. It exposes the held snapshot, the held range, the resolved text, and a `clear` callback.

**The overlay component.** A thin component renders the frozen snapshot: one element per line, monospace, positioned over the terminal's viewport and sized to fill it, with the selected run of each line wrapped in a highlight span — the same way the editor paints its own selection, at `web/src/editor/render.tsx:98`, where a span carries `editor-sel` when it falls inside the selected range. It is rendered only while a selection is being made or held, and is a descendant of the surface's registered container — see the design decision above; a right-click that lands outside that container resolves no terminal selection.

Its styling joins `web/src/theme.css` beside the terminal rules at `:566`: the terminal's own `--terminal-bg` for the frozen body, and **`--editor-selection` for the highlight** (`web/src/theme.css:684`, `.editor-sel`), which is already defined once per theme (`:22`, `:41`, `:59`, `:77`, `:95`, `:113`). No new palette entry is added — the app has exactly one selection colour and this is it, even though its name says editor. The container gets `position: relative` if it does not already establish a positioning context.

**`useXterm` returns state instead of a bare function.** Today it returns a `focus` callback (`web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:101`); it returns an object carrying that callback plus the layer's state. All three call sites change: `HarnessTab.tsx:39` and `ShellTab.tsx:28` destructure `focus` for their imperative handles, `TerminalCard.tsx:25` ignores it as it does today, and each renders the overlay component inside its existing container div. This is a deliberate churn of three files rather than an imperative DOM mount from inside the hook, which would put rendering in a hook and break the layering `ai/guidelines/react-code-organization.md` requires.

**The bridge answers the layer first.** `useXterm` currently registers the `Terminal` itself as the `TerminalAccess` (`:40`, `registerTerminalSelection(container, term)`) because a `Terminal` happens to satisfy that two-method shape. It instead registers a small object that answers from the layer when it holds a selection and delegates to the terminal otherwise. `terminal-selection.ts` itself does not change — this is why the bridge was worth building as a two-method contract. This one change is what makes **Chat about this**, its Cmd+I shortcut, and the whole plugin path work with no edit anywhere else.

The alternative is to put the text on the overlay as a `data-editor-selection` attribute, which `editorSelectionText` already reads off the nearest ancestor (`web/src/context-menu/default-menu-target.ts:32`–`:35`, as `EditorTab.tsx:139` supplies it). It is rejected: it would label a terminal's selection as an editor's, and that label is exactly what decides whether the menu offers Copy (`default-menu-target.ts:64`–`:67`). The decision above is that a terminal's menu keeps offering no Copy, so the source must stay `'terminal'` — an attribute choice must not be what quietly changes it.

**Three clearing triggers beyond Escape and the plain click.** The layer clears when the surface's tab is switched away from, when the terminal resizes, and when the PTY exits. Each has an existing hook to hang off rather than a new mechanism: the `ResizeObserver` already installed at `web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:89`–`:90` covers the resize; the exit is already surfaced to the surfaces that care (`harness.status === 'exited'` at `web/src/harness/HarnessTab.tsx:68`, `entry.status` at `web/src/shared/transcript/TerminalCard.tsx:32`); and a tab switch unmounts or hides the surface, so the layer clears on the same teardown path that already unregisters the selection bridge (`useXterm.ts:96`). Where a surface is hidden rather than unmounted, the clear is driven from the same prop the surface already reads to know it is inactive — no new state is introduced to detect any of the three.

**`macOptionClickForcesSelection` is removed.** The option at `web/src/shared/terminal/useXterm.ts:36` — and the four-line comment above it explaining why it was needed — comes out, since Shift+drag now selects on macOS too and xterm's own selection service should no longer take a drag from the harness. The comment is replaced by one naming the layer as what supplies the gesture instead, so the next reader does not restore the option.

**Two branches in the key handler.** Inside `attachCustomKeyEventHandler` (`:58`), before the existing copy branch: Escape with a held selection clears it and returns false; with nothing held it falls through untouched. The existing copy branch (`:68`) checks the layer before `term.hasSelection()`, copying the layer's text when it holds one — and, per the answers, does **not** clear it. Order matters: the Shift+Enter translation (`:60`) and the surface's own `keyFilter` (`:65`) keep their current precedence, since an overlay-owned chord and a tab-switch chord must still outrank anything this feature adds.

**Specs.** `product/specs/harness.md` § "Selecting and copying terminal text" (`:388`) is rewritten around the layer: the gesture on every platform, the freeze, the visible-screen extent, what clears it (Escape, a plain click, a tab switch, a resize, an exit), that typing still reaches the harness, and that copy and **Chat about this** both leave it in place. Its current Option-versus-Shift paragraph (`:392`–`:395`) goes, since there is no longer a platform difference. Because the layer reaches every xterm surface, `product/specs/shell.md` (PTY takeover, terminal cards) and `product/specs/ssh-tab.md` point at that section rather than restating it. `product/specs/context-menu.md:22` is updated for the new gesture; `:32`'s no-Copy rule stands unchanged and is left alone. `product/specs/keyboard-navigation.md` records Escape's new conditional meaning inside a terminal surface. `documentation/user-documentation/` gains the gesture wherever it currently documents copying from a harness.

## Tests

Colocated as `web/src/**/*.test.ts(x)` (vitest project `client`), beside the existing terminal tests.

- **The model module** (new): the snapshot is the `rows` lines from `viewportY`, not the whole buffer — the case that fails if the server-side reader's loop is copied without its `scrollback: 0` assumption, and the one to write first; trailing blank lines are dropped and an empty buffer yields an empty list; a point maps to the expected cell for a given container size and `cols`/`rows`; a point outside the container clamps to the grid edge; a backwards drag normalizes to the same range as the forwards one; a zero-length range resolves to empty text and counts as no selection; a multi-line range joins with newlines and trims each line's trailing spaces; a single-line range takes only the columns between the two cells.
- **The layer hook** (new): Shift+pointerdown takes a snapshot, starts a selection, and calls both `preventDefault` and `stopPropagation`; a plain pointerdown clears a held selection and is likewise consumed; a pointerup outside the container still ends the drag; a second Shift+pointerdown re-snapshots and replaces; `clear` drops both the snapshot and the range.
- **The freeze is immune to output** — the case the whole design exists for: writing to the terminal after a selection is held changes neither the snapshot, the range, nor the resolved text.
- **The three clearing triggers**: a resize, the PTY exiting, and the surface going inactive each clear a held selection and drop the overlay.
- **`web/src/shared/terminal/terminal-selection.test.ts`**: a registered surface holding a layer selection answers with the layer's text; with none it falls back to the terminal's own selection; the innermost-container rule still holds.
- **`web/src/harness/HarnessTab.test.tsx`**: a held selection is what the copy chord copies and what `terminalSelectionText` resolves for a right-click landing on the overlay; the resolved source stays `'terminal'`, so the menu still offers no Copy; the copy chord does not clear it; Escape with a selection clears it and does not reach the PTY; **Escape with no selection does reach the PTY**; typing while a selection is held still reaches the PTY.
- **`web/src/ShellTab.test.tsx`** and **`web/src/shared/transcript/TerminalCard.test.tsx`**: the gesture works on surfaces that never took the mouse, which is what proves the layer is mounted for every xterm surface rather than harness tabs alone; both files already mock the hook as `useXterm: vi.fn(() => () => {})` (`web/src/ShellTab.test.tsx:9`, `web/src/shared/transcript/TerminalCard.test.tsx:8`) — a bare function, so the return-shape change breaks them until the mock returns the new object. This is the change most likely to red the suite for a reason unrelated to the feature.
- No server-side tests are expected: `defaultMenuSelectionAction`, `runDefaultMenuSelectionAction`, and the conversations plugin are untouched and already covered by `src/plugins/default-menu.test.ts`.

## Out of scope

Boundaries the backlog text already draws, plus the ones the answered questions settle.

- **Changing what the harness does with the mouse.** No harness is configured, patched, or asked to stop reporting mouse events; the layer intercepts the gesture on Janissary's side.
- **Any new consumer of a selection.** Copy and **Chat about this** are the two the backlog text names. No send-to-another-tab, no search-for-this, no save-to-file.
- **Block/column selection.** The backlog text describes an ordinary drag.
- **Editing, re-flowing, or annotating the selected text.** What is copied is what was on screen.
- **Any on-screen indicator, hint, or mode banner.** Settled above: the highlight is the whole affordance.
- **Keyboard-driven selection.** No chord starts, extends, or moves a selection; the gesture is Shift plus a drag.
- **Scrolling, searching, or navigating inside the frozen overlay.** It is a still image of one screenful of text with a selection on it, not a pager. Selecting across more than one screen means scrolling the terminal first and dragging again.
- **Restoring a selection after it clears.** Nothing is remembered across a tab switch, a resize, or an exit; the next selection is made from scratch.
- **Preserving the harness's own colours in the overlay.** The snapshot is text; styling, bold, and ANSI colour are not carried across.

# Export a chart as SVG and as the CSV of its plotted rows

A card offered PNG and a PDF, and the PDF is a rasterized image of the same SVG at twice the size. A chart destined for a document, a review or a repository therefore came out as a raster at one fixed scale — it blurs when scaled and cannot be edited or diffed — and the rows behind it were reachable only by reading the table on screen and copying them by hand, which is the one export a screen-reader user most needs. Vega's view exposes the same three shapes of export (`toSVG`, `toCanvas(scaleFactor)`, `toImageURL('png' | 'svg', scaleFactor)`), `vl-convert` ships `vl2svg`, `vl2png`, `vl2pdf` and `vl2url`, and Datawrapper's footer offers "Get the data" as a locale-formatted CSV of the visualised data beside the image downloads.

- `exportSvg` in `web/src/plugins/visualizations/export/download.ts` serializes the on-screen element with the theme custom properties already resolved into literal values, reusing the `chartProperties` list the PNG and PDF paths depend on. An SVG carrying `var(--accent)` renders black in anything that is not this tab, which is the reason the resolution exists and the reason the list is published for the style contract to hold.
- `csvOf` lives in its own `export/csv.ts` because it is the one export that is not a picture: it is quoted, it is text, and the rules are its own. RFC 4180 quoting for a value holding a delimiter, a quote or a line break, with a doubled quote inside — a value holding a comma is the common case and the one a naive join turns into three columns. A value beginning `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is quoted as well, because every spreadsheet reads those as a formula and data out of somebody's log is not one. A value that is not a finite number is written as nothing rather than as the word `NaN`, which would be a file claiming a measurement it does not have.
- The rows come from `dataTableFor`, not from the source: a chart over a transformed table is a chart of the transformed table, and a CSV of the source would answer a different question than the picture does. A leading `#` comment names the chart, its transformations and where the data came from, which every spreadsheet and every diff tool treats as a comment.
- `csv.ts` declares the table shape structurally rather than importing it from `chart/describe.ts`. A tab plugin reaches its own modules only through the shared contract, and the import-boundary rule is right: two structural types need no contract between them.
- Two buttons on the card beside the two existing ones, per chart rather than per tab, because a reader thinking about a picture thinks about one picture.

`web/src/plugins/visualizations/export/csv.test.ts` covers the header and column line, a value holding a comma, a quote and a line break, the four formula characters, an ordinary number and word, a value that is not a number, and a column name holding a comma. `VisualizationChartCard.test.tsx` asserts both buttons sit on the card's own action row, and `VisualizationTab.test.tsx`'s "no dropdown and no tab-level control" case now counts six rather than four, which is the product decision this feature is built on being restated by its own test. The spec's line about the four controls becomes six, with what the two new ones are for.

# technical-debt

## ready

* Gather the command-input feature into `web/src/command-input/`: `CommandInput.tsx`, `CommandArea.tsx`, `useCommandHistoryRecall.ts`, `command-caret-lines.ts`, `command-completion.ts`, `ghost-suggestion.ts`, `textarea-splice.ts`, `command-interceptions.ts`, and `useCommandBarSubmit.ts` all sit loose in the flat `web/src/` root alongside every other feature's files, which is §1 (organize by feature, not by file type — a directory name should name a piece of the product). Grepping each one shows a closed cluster rather than shared utilities: `command-caret-lines.ts`, `command-completion.ts`, `ghost-suggestion.ts`, `textarea-splice.ts`, and `useCommandHistoryRecall.ts` have exactly one consumer apiece, `CommandInput.tsx`, and `command-interceptions.ts` has exactly one, `useCommandBarSubmit.ts` — single-consumer helpers that §2 (colocate; promote to shared only on the second consumer) says belong beside their consumer rather than in the shared root. The cost is that generic names like `textarea-splice.ts` sit in a 125-file root advertising themselves for reuse, so a second feature imports one and cements a boundary nobody chose, and an author changing caret or ghost-suggestion handling has to grep the whole root to learn nothing else depends on it. Move the nine files with their colocated tests into `web/src/command-input/`, flip their intra-cluster imports to `./` and their imports of `icons.ts`, `ws.ts`, and `drop-handles.ts` to `../`, and repoint the three outside importers: `AgentTabBody.tsx` for `CommandArea`, `InactiveAgentTabBody.tsx` for `CommandInput`, and `App.tsx` for `useCommandBarSubmit`. Leave `history.ts` and `populate-command-line.ts` where they are — `history.ts` is consumed by `App.tsx` and `populate-command-line.ts` by both `useTaskPicker.ts` and `useProfilePicker.ts`. It is all import-path churn with no behavior change. Severity: **medium**.

* Extract the agent-tab protocol wiring duplicated by `web/src/AgentTabBody.tsx` and `web/src/InactiveAgentTabBody.tsx` into a plain intents module beside them: both components build the same five `client.send` calls inline in their JSX — `openFileNavigatorFor`, `launchAgentFor`, and `openTranscriptFor` on `AgentTabMeta` (`AgentTabBody.tsx:107-109` and `InactiveAgentTabBody.tsx:29-31`), `toggleCollapse` on `Transcript` (lines 123 and 45), and `openAcpTranscript` on `StatusPanels` (lines 133 and 54) — differing only in whether the label is read from `current` or `tab`. That is §5 (components render; they do not decide — protocol calls belong in a hook or a plain module beneath the component). The cost is that the method names and param shapes are written twice, so renaming a server method means finding both copies and a miss is silent, and neither wiring can be exercised without rendering an entire tab body including `Transcript`, `StatusPanels`, and `CommandInput`. The pattern to copy already sits in the same directory: `web/src/transcript-intents.ts` takes the client and returns named callbacks precisely so "the renderers take these callbacks instead of the protocol client, so they stay presentational and the command strings below are the only place that knows how an intent reaches the server." Add `agent-tab-intents.ts` in that same shape, taking `(client, label)` and returning the five callbacks, and have both bodies call it. Only those two files change and neither's props move, so none of the files that render them are affected. Severity: **medium**.

* Route the four hand-built `connectionsButton` literals through the `statusButton` helper that already exists: `web/src/InactiveAgentTabBody.tsx:32`, `web/src/AgentTabBody.tsx:110`, `web/src/HarnessTabLayer.tsx:55`, and `web/src/editor/useEditorConnections.ts:29` each construct the same `{ hasContent, onEnter, onLeave, onClick }` object by hand out of a `useStatusWindows` result, while `web/src/status-button.ts` exports `statusButton(hasContent, window)` returning exactly that shape — `InactiveAgentTabBody.tsx` even calls the helper for its `scheduleButton` on the very next prop and then rebuilds the connections one inline. That is §5 (components render; they do not decide): mapping a status window's handlers onto a button's props is data shaping that a pure module already owns. The cost is that adding or renaming a field on `StatusWindowButtonProps` means editing five places instead of one, and three of the four copies are buried in JSX where no test reaches them directly. Replace each literal with a `statusButton(...)` call, keeping `HarnessTabLayer.tsx`'s `scheduleOnly ? undefined :` guard around its call. Four files change and no props, exports, or types move. Severity: **low**.

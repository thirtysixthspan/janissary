* Validate the WebSocket message envelope before dispatching it: `src/index.ts:107` turns every inbound frame into a `ClientMessage` with a bare `JSON.parse(raw.toString()) as ClientMessage` assertion, and `handle` in `src/message-handler.ts` immediately reads `message.method` and destructures `message.params.*` on the strength of that assertion alone — so a well-formed-JSON frame carrying a missing or wrong-typed `params` reaches controller and manager methods as `undefined`/`NaN` instead of being rejected at the edge. The codebase already has the pattern to copy (`isHistoryEntry` in `src/global-history.ts`, `isAgentState` in `src/agent/state.ts`); the ingress principle 9 says every request passes through is the one place without it. Add a narrow envelope guard (recognized `method`, `params` present and an object) and drop frames that fail it. While there, stop the fallthrough at `src/message-handler.ts:156-157` from replying `'ok'` to a method the switch never handled. Severity: **medium**.

* Unify the three dispatch styles in `src/message-handler.ts`: the single 145-line switch routes some methods through a `Controller` method (`controller.closeTab`), some by reaching past the controller into a manager (`controller.managers.tab.setActiveTab`, `controller.managers.questions.answer`, `controller.managers.schedule.clearAll`), and some by calling a feature module directly (`setClientLayout`, `openTranscriptFor`, `openAcpTranscript`, `listPersonas`, `editorSuggest`, `closeConnection`) — with three comments stating outright that the bypass exists "to keep controller.ts under its line-size limit". That lets a line budget rather than the design decide where a method's entry point lives, and leaves no single place to answer what happens when the client sends a given method. Pick one rule and apply it to every case, so the 200-line limit gets satisfied by splitting the handler rather than by routing around the controller. Severity: **medium**.
